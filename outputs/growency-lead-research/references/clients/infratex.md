# Infratex: research playbook

Authority: [the user-supplied sales/campaign brief](../../assets/briefs/infratex.original.txt), received 21 October 2026. Read it for full segment details and claim caveats. The brief overrides this summary.

## Scope

**Europe only.** Verify country and the relevant operating entity. Europe does not mean EU membership or establish data residency. Put ambiguous regional/parent-subsidiary situations into review instead of assuming geography from the language of the website.

Keep separate campaigns and language for:

1. Financial software: first priority; lending/credit, CRE underwriting, financial research/data as distinct subsegments.
2. Insurance software: alongside finance; commercial underwriting/submissions.
3. AI implementation partners: technical consultancies with relevant deployed work or live projects.

The brief additionally describes direct B2B manufacturers/distributors and logistics. Preserve this as a separate optional campaign; ask which segment to activate rather than mixing direct finance/operations buyers into the three initial campaigns. Do not use European targeting to discard a non-European **customer proof point**; proof geography and prospect geography are different fields.

## Commercial question

Which exact document type enters this company's real workflow, what output needs to preserve its meaning, and who owns evaluating or integrating it? A document workflow is essential evidence. A funding event or “we do AI” tagline alone is insufficient.

## Explain the help simply

Every Infratex research brief must answer **how Infratex could help this particular company**, not merely describe its industry or suggest a vague outreach angle. Use about five short bullets, with a dedicated **How Infratex could help** bullet.

Make that bullet one plain sentence, usually 12–25 words: **Infratex could take [specific information] from [their document type] and put it into [their useful output or task].** Adapt this naturally. It must make sense to a rep without a technical background.

- Anchor the proposed help in an observed document workflow. Name useful fields where known, such as tenant names and rents, borrower revenue and debt, or claims dates and amounts.
- Explain one practical job: fill the fields their software needs, prepare a comparison table, or make document information searchable. Do not list the whole Infratex product.
- Use “could” for the proposed application. State existing retyping, errors or delays as facts only when the source establishes them. Otherwise leave the current process as the main question to check.
- Avoid jargon such as “optimize ingestion pipelines,” “canonical schema,” “verification layer,” “unlock efficiencies” or “leverage AI.” “Extract the figures from their reports” is clearer.
- Do not promise ready-made integrations, complete underwriting/approval systems, savings or accuracy. If no relevant document task is established, say **No clear document use case found** rather than inventing a benefit.

Default five bullets: what the company does; the exact document workflow and source; a useful signal or likely owner; **How Infratex could help** in plain language; fit and the main unknown. Keep the normal quick research budget. See [three illustrative briefs](../../assets/infratex-research-examples.md) for the intended simplicity; their facts are fictional, not lead evidence.

## Research specifics

Find product/help pages, engineering jobs, case studies, launches and integration docs that name the input document and its downstream use. Record:

- Exact document type and whether use is current, announced or merely a hypothetical marketing example.
- Input formats and observable process: extraction, search, structured schema, review/correction or export.
- Where output goes: product feature, underwriting/credit process, client app or ERP.
- Documented current tools/process; distinguish the company using a tool from merely writing about it.
- Review controls, difficult scans, row/period/unit requirements **only when evidenced**. Otherwise turn them into a question.
- Technical owner and likely integration responsibility; for partners, the delivery/commercial owner and whether a live project is confirmed.
- Volume, current pain, deadline and evaluation readiness if public; leave them unknown rather than deriving volume from company headcount.
- Counterevidence: generic AI positioning, no document-heavy work, wrong buying team, mature satisfactory current solution, parent procurement or no live partner project.

**Worth mentioning:** one named document workflow or precise technical requirement. **Usually ignore as opener:** generic funding, accelerator status, unrelated AI activity. Keep segment vocabulary exact: a loss run is not a rent roll; a lender is not automatically a financial software provider.

## Offer and claims

Infratex handles extracting/searching document content and structured output, API/SDK integration and tailored deployments/workflows. A parser does not equal an entire underwriting or invoice-approval system. Direct workflow packages in the brief are proposals; verify delivery/integrations/support before claiming every feature exists.

Proposed comparison: one document type and agreed schema; up to 20 documents, **both** capped at 200 total pages; score correctness, missing information, processing time and estimated cost, including failures. Start with public or authorized redacted samples. Free scope and three-business-day turnaround are proposals subject to adoption, agreed scope/data arrangements and capacity; do not promise them until confirmed. Alternative CTA: a short technical walkthrough on a public sample. Give something concrete before requesting a follow-up call.

VPC/on-prem options may be discussed subject to the specific deployment; do not infer that every platform component runs privately or promise certifications, hosting geography, retention, throughput, SLA or discounts. Infratex owns technical/security/commercial confirmation and private-data transfer arrangements. Do not collect private prospect documents during lead research.

Client-supplied proof is attributed, not independently verified here:

- Swedish private-credit firm, about $4B AUM: customized borrower-data ingestion/dashboard/query workflow. This AUM is the customer's scale, not Infratex revenue or savings.
- Italian legal client: difficult parsing work. The referenced screenshot/exact wording was not supplied in this text; do not invent a verbatim testimonial or repeat “12 parsers” as independently verified.
- Canadian consultancy: private deployment and a more cost-effective extraction setup as reported by the client; no unsupported percentage or universal superiority claim.
- Tech Europe Foundry participation / prior Ignition special prize: credibility context, not proof of product accuracy.

Use scoped facts and offer a comparison. Do not repeat “unmatched accuracy” or “no parser can match” as established universal facts. Partner revenue share/resale/white-label/exclusivity arrangements require established terms; a live project opportunity and potential future partnership are separate judgments.
