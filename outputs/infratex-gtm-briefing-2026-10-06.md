**Infratex GTM and outbound sales briefing**

Prepared for Growency · Research date: 6 October 2026

**Recommendation:** Lead with a document-extraction benchmark for software companies serving financial workflows. Test insurance software and AI implementation partners as separate comparison groups. Convert successful evaluations into a bounded paid pilot and recurring production contract. Prioritize qualified meetings, production conversion, and gross profit alongside positive replies.

This is a proposed first-sales motion, based on public product documentation, pricing, competitor materials, buyer websites, and outreach research. Infratex’s actual customers, revenue, processing costs, security evidence, production reliability, and historical conversion rates were not supplied or verified. Recommendations and commercial figures below are hypotheses to validate, not established performance or approved offers.

**1. Four GTM ideas, ranked**

| Priority | Idea | Buyer and opening offer | Commercial rationale | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Financial-document extraction for software products | CTO or engineering lead at lending, credit, financial research, or CRE software companies; compare extraction on difficult financial PDFs | A recurring product workload can expand across the buyer’s customers | Document volume, incumbent performance, and Infratex’s actual advantage |
| 2 | Insurance submission tables | Engineering/product owners at underwriting software firms; benchmark loss-run tables or schedules of values | Specific document types make relevance and evaluation concrete | Insurance-specific accuracy, supported formats, and security requirements |
| 3 | Document-processing infrastructure for AI consultancies | Founder, delivery director, or AI practice lead; evaluate the ingestion component of one live client project | One relationship may generate multiple implementations | Partners may have no live project, want custom work, or already own competing tooling |
| 4 | Rescue difficult documents in an existing pipeline | Teams with a verified parser evaluation, new document class, or extraction issue; test Infratex on the difficult subset | A limited integration reduces switching effort | A small exception stream may never generate enough revenue for outbound |

Ideas 1–3 are audience choices. Idea 4 is an entry tactic usable within them, not a fourth broad campaign.

Start with financial software as the leading hypothesis. Do not treat this ranking as measured market demand. Insurance can become the first choice if Infratex already has stronger customer evidence there. Partners can become the first choice if direct workloads are too small but implementation support is strong.

**2. What the research establishes**

Infratex presents itself as document infrastructure for AI applications. Its homepage emphasizes complex tables and reports a self-run PulseBench-Tab result of 0.9636, plus an anonymous fintech testimonial. These are useful starting points for evaluation, not independently verified customer outcomes. [Infratex product page](https://www.infratex.ai/)

The documentation describes an API for parsing, structured extraction, indexing, and cited answers, with Python and Node SDKs. It distinguishes Standard and Max from the in-house, self-deployable Infratex-Phi model. That supports targeting technical product teams, while making deployment and model-specific claims precise. [Infratex documentation](https://www.infratex.ai/documentation)

Public monthly plans are €25, €50, and €500. A credit is €0.005; Standard parsing consumes one credit per page, while Max and Phi consume three. Extraction and other operations add usage. Higher-volume/custom arrangements are available. This means outbound economics must be qualified early. [Infratex pricing](https://www.infratex.ai/pricing)

There is visible demand for this category. Reducto publishes an insurance claims case study with Elysian, and Extend markets financial-document processing and publishes customer evidence involving Brex. These are competitor-reported examples of purchasing activity; their outcomes are not proof that Infratex can reproduce them. [Reducto/Elysian](https://reducto.ai/blog/reducto-elysian-case-study), [Extend financial-services research](https://www.extend.ai/resources/financial-statement-apis-lending)

**The competitive implication:** buyers already have options. Infratex needs a clear improvement on a defined workload and a practical adoption path. “We use AI to process documents” will not establish either.

Proposed positioning:

> Infratex helps financial software teams turn difficult financial PDFs into structured data, with source evidence their team can check. Start with a measured comparison on the documents that create the most review work.

The promise is a testable process. Savings and accuracy improvements become sales claims only after they are measured.

Request the dataset revision, scorer settings, model versions, dates, raw outputs, missing-output treatment, and reproduction instructions. A table-structure score is not equivalent to financial-field accuracy or end-to-end workflow reliability. **4. Ideal customer profile and qualification**

Initial geography: US and UK corporate businesses, in separate campaign cohorts. This is an operating assumption for English-language outreach and manageable coverage; confirm Infratex’s commercial and hosting support before launch. Do not automatically reuse the same rules in other jurisdictions.

Initial company filter: approximately 20–250 employees, a live software product, and an identifiable engineering owner. Employee count is a search shortcut, not evidence of workload or budget. Include smaller firms when a concrete project and processing volume justify it.

Primary financial subsegments:

- Lending and credit workflow software processing financial statements and supporting documents.
- CRE underwriting software handling rent rolls and operating statements.
- Financial research/data products ingesting recurring reports with complex tables.

Keep these subsegments separately tagged. Their documents, vocabulary, and economics differ.

A prospect needs four things to justify sales effort: a recurring document workflow, a material reason to improve it, a person who can evaluate/integrate, and credible commercial value. Confirm volume on the first call; do not invent it from company size.

At list prices before discounts and additional operations, 100,000 Standard pages represent about €500 of monthly usage. If only 10% are routed to Infratex, the applicable volume is 10,000 pages. Record total pages and likely Infratex pages separately. An attractive exception-handling conversation can still be a poor revenue opportunity.

**5. Evidence-led prospecting**

Prioritize signals in this order:

1. Explicitly described extraction problems, a vendor evaluation, or a new difficult document type.
2. A live product feature that depends on financial/insurance documents, plus an identifiable owner.
3. A relevant launch, implementation case study, or engineering vacancy describing document ingestion.
4. Generic funding or AI activity only as secondary context.

A job mentioning OCR is evidence of relevant work, not proof that their current system fails. A product page establishes fit, not buying intent.

For each account, record: company/domain, segment, source URL and date, relevant workflow, signal strength, buyer role, current provider if publicly confirmed, estimated fit, volume status, geography, email source/verification, outreach status, suppression status, and reason for exclusion. Use “unknown” wherever evidence is absent.

Suggested manual score: document fit 0–3; evidence of a current initiative/problem 0–3; plausible commercial scale 0–2; reachable technical owner 0–2. Prioritize 7+/10, but retain the evidence behind the number. Re-score after discovery.

**6. The offer and conversion path**

Use a three-stage commercial path. The scope and prices here are proposals for the founders to validate.

**Stage A: Free, bounded document benchmark.** Offer up to 20 representative documents, capped at 200 pages, for one document class and one agreed output. Deliver a short scorecard, examples of correct and incorrect output, and a cost estimate. Include typical documents and difficult cases; use public or authorized redacted samples initially. Aim for delivery within three business days after scope and data handling are agreed, only if staffing supports it.

Set a default internal cap of roughly one engineer-hour per free evaluation and ten evaluations per week initially. If the work requires custom connectors, extensive schema work, or data preparation, scope a paid evaluation. The caps are planning choices; measure actual effort.

Do not require confidential uploads in the first cold email. First earn interest, establish relevance, and agree an appropriate transfer route. Do not imply that we have already tested the prospect’s files.

**Stage B: Paid validation pilot.** Proposed test price: €1,500–€3,000 for two weeks, one workflow, a capped document set, agreed fields, and a clear deliverable. State integration/support limits. Any credit against a later contract must be included in margin calculations. This package is not currently verified as an Infratex offer.

The pilot should use an unseen holdout set, preserve errors and failed runs in the denominator, and compare the buyer’s incumbent configuration fairly. Do not cherry-pick only failed files when estimating normal production cost or accuracy.

Measure critical-field correctness, row completeness, invented/misassigned values, source traceability, document success rate, review time, processing latency, total cost, and integration effort. Buyer-specific acceptance criteria should govern progression. For consequential workflows, unverified outputs must remain reviewable; do not promise fully autonomous decisions.

**Stage C: Production.** Seek accounts with a plausible €12,000–€36,000 annual commercial value through justified usage, support, or deployment requirements. This is a targeting hypothesis, not an approved price increase. Keep entry plans available. Specify included usage and separately scoped services to avoid double charging or hiding fees.

A higher contract cannot simply repackage the €500 public plan at a premium. The buyer must receive additional, deliverable value. Price private infrastructure only after its operating and support costs are understood.

**7. Cold-email strategy**

The initial message should contain one observed fact, one relevant workflow issue framed conditionally, one concrete offer, and one easy next step. Start around 60–100 words before the necessary footer. Use plain language and avoid speculative savings, invented familiarity, attachments, and false “Re:” subjects.

Gong’s research supports testing interest/value offers in cold outreach rather than immediately requesting calendar time. It does not establish an Infratex-specific conversion rate, and the strongest choice must be tested on this audience. [Gong executive email research](https://www.gong.io/blog/do-execs-really-reply-to-cold-email-here-s-what-the-data-says), [Gong CTA study](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings)

For all drafts, add the real business identity, required postal/contact details, privacy information where applicable, clear commercial identification, and an easy opt-out. No invented addresses or customer proof.

**Suggested sequence:** Day 1 initial offer; Day 4 share one relevant example or explain one evaluation criterion; Day 9 concise final follow-up. Stop the sequence on any reply and stop all marketing on an objection. Do not repeatedly contact multiple colleagues in parallel. Start with one relevant person; use a second sequentially when appropriate, and stop account-wide automation when a conversation begins.

An example follow-up: “The comparison would check row completeness as well as whether the output is valid JSON. Would the scorecard format help you decide whether a test is worthwhile?”

A final follow-up: “I’ll leave this here. If [document type] extraction becomes a priority, happy to revisit the comparison.”

For high-fit accounts, a manual LinkedIn touch can add context, subject to platform and local rules. Do not interpret a profile view, connection, or email open as purchase intent.

**8. Turn positive replies into qualified meetings**

Assign one reply owner and a backup. Aim to respond within the same working day, ideally within two business hours during agreed coverage. Provide the item requested first. Ask one lightweight qualifying question, then suggest a short technical scoping call with two actual available times.

“Happy to share the scorecard. Which document type is most relevant, and is this a recurring production workflow? If useful, we can spend 15 minutes agreeing the sample and what a successful result would mean.”

A qualified meeting should include the right owner or a credible route to them, a relevant recurring workflow, a material problem or active evaluation, plausible commercial scale, and an agreed next step. A request for information is an interest signal, not automatically a qualified meeting.

Suggested 20-minute first call: understand the workflow and incumbent; quantify volume and review effort; inspect a representative example; agree evaluation success criteria; confirm who handles technical, security, and commercial decisions. End with a dated action and owner.

| Objection | Useful response |
| --- | --- |
| “We already use another parser.” | Establish whether a new document class or measurable gap warrants any comparison. If the incumbent meets requirements, deprioritize. |
| “Send information.” | Send the requested sample/scorecard and ask which document class matters. Do not count it as meeting intent. |
| “We can build this.” | Compare the full operating workload, including maintenance and review, against an actual build estimate. |
| “We cannot share files.” | Begin with public/redacted examples; involve the security owner before private data. |
| “Your website is much cheaper.” | Explain precisely what usage or services create the proposed contract value. If none do, use the public plan. |
| “No time to integrate.” | Offer a limited offline evaluation; give a realistic integration estimate after inspection. |

**9. Six-week execution and experiment design**

Suggested owners are role assignments, not existing commitments.

| Timing | Suggested owner | Work and expected outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Infratex founder + technical lead | Verify product claims, costs, deployment/security answers, sample capacity, and pilot scope; remove promises that cannot be delivered |
| Week 1 | Growency strategy lead | Prepare a scorecard, concise segment-specific page/one-pager, approved message set, reply process, and measurement definitions |
| Week 2 | Growency researcher + campaign owner | Research 50 accounts per segment across financial software, insurance software, and partners; contact one primary person per account after quality review |
| Weeks 3–4 | Growency campaign owner + Infratex engineer | Complete follow-ups and evaluations; add up to another 50 accounts per segment if targeting and delivery remain sound |
| Week 5 | Growency analyst + founders | Compare the completed cohorts on positive replies, held qualified meetings, pilot progression, and staff cost; investigate the strongest and weakest cells |
| Week 6 | Founders + Growency | Concentrate the next cohort on the strongest commercially credible segment, progress paid pilots, and produce permissioned proof |

Initial ceiling: approximately 300 researched accounts, with at most two relevant contacts per account contacted sequentially. This is a resource plan, not a quota that overrides relevance or sending constraints. It could produce up to 600 unique recipients, but actual volume should be lower when conversations, exclusions, or opt-outs warrant it.

Keep the offer format and follow-up timing reasonably consistent across segments, while adapting document-specific language. Segment comparisons are directional because the audiences differ. Do not split this first cohort into many subject-line, copy, CTA, geography, and persona experiments.

After finding a plausible segment, test one variable within it—for example, a sample scorecard versus a direct benchmark offer. Randomize by account and hold other factors stable. A small number of replies does not establish a statistically reliable winner. If both cohorts remain sparse, gather qualitative feedback before scaling.

**Decision rules:** investigate any delivery deterioration immediately. If a completed 100-recipient segment produces no positive replies, inspect relevance, offer, and delivery before adding volume; this is a diagnostic trigger, not proof that the market is absent. If replies produce few relevant meetings, tighten the offer and qualification. If meetings produce no pilots, inspect urgency, trust, product performance, and price. If pilots succeed technically but do not convert, inspect commercial value and buying authority.

**10. Measurement and commercial economics**

Track the funnel using consistent denominators:

- Positive-reply rate: unique people expressing relevant interest / unique people with at least one delivered message. Separate generic information requests from substantive interest; exclude automated replies, rejections, and unsubscribes.
- Booked-meeting rate: unique prospects booking / unique delivered recipients. Also report positive-to-booked conversion.
- Held-meeting rate: meetings held / meetings booked.
- Qualified-meeting rate: qualified meetings / held meetings, plus qualified meetings / unique delivered recipients.
- Paid-pilot rate, production-win rate, time to revenue, customer acquisition cost, and contribution after delivery costs.

Track account-level results alongside contact results to prevent multiple colleagues inflating opportunity counts. Treat open rates as secondary and unreliable, not an optimization target. Belkins’ latest report explicitly changed its reply-rate denominator, illustrating why attractive headline benchmarks cannot be compared without checking definitions. [Belkins methodology](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)

The following is sensitivity analysis for **600 unique delivered recipients**, not a forecast, industry benchmark, or promise:

| Scenario | Positive replies | Booked meetings | Held meetings | Qualified held meetings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weak response | 0.5% = 3 | 33% of positive ≈ 1 | 70% of booked ≈ 0.7 | 50% of held ≈ 0.35 |
| Working hypothesis | 2% = 12 | 50% = 6 | 80% = 4.8 | 60% = 2.9 |
| Strong response | 5% = 30 | 60% = 18 | 85% = 15.3 | 70% = 10.7 |

Fractional outcomes are expected values used for planning; actual outcomes are whole counts and may be zero. Replace all assumptions with Infratex campaign data. Bookings are not revenue, and production sales may fall outside the six-week test.

**Infratex economics:** recurring revenue minus inference, infrastructure, support, review, and delivery costs must support acquisition. A simple planning example: €6,000 acquisition spend generating six qualified meetings, with a hypothetical 20% eventual win rate, implies 1.2 expected wins and €5,000 acquisition cost per win. Add €1,000 unfunded onboarding cost: €6,000 total investment per customer.

At an assumed 70% recurring gross margin, payback is approximately 171 months at €50/month revenue, 17 months at €500/month, and 5.7 months at €1,500/month. These figures exclude churn and discounting, assume stable revenue and margin, and are illustrative. They show why high-touch acquisition must be matched to sufficient contract value. Paid onboarding changes the model; unused capacity and support overruns can worsen it.

Estimate buyer value using documented review-time savings, avoided processing costs, and verified engineering effort. Do not price against an invented “AI ROI” percentage. Record source assumptions with the buyer and avoid counting the same saved time twice.

**Growency economics:** calculate fee income less research, campaign operations, data/tools, reply handling, strategy, and management time. Keep Infratex’s technical evaluation work explicitly assigned and funded. Use a setup fee and retainer that cover baseline delivery, with an optional bonus for an agreed qualified held meeting or collected commercial outcome. Do not base the entire engagement on unqualified bookings.

Example only: if monthly Growency delivery cost is €2,500 and desired gross margin is 50%, the minimum fee is €2,500 / (1 − 0.50) = €5,000 before tax and any pass-through costs. This is a formula illustration, not a recommended quote without cost data. Define duplicates, existing customers, no-shows, attribution, and qualification in the client agreement.

**11. Deliverability and launch requirements**

Use a truthful, stable sender identity with authenticated domains. Configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC; verify alignment and actual delivery. Use legitimate infrastructure, verified business addresses, modest initial volume, and gradual changes. Avoid tracking pixels in the initial campaign, bulk attachments, misleading domains, and artificial engagement. Follow the sending provider’s acceptable-use terms; authentication alone does not make unsolicited mail acceptable to every provider.

Google’s requirements differ for ordinary and bulk senders to personal Gmail accounts. Its guidance recommends spam rates below 0.1% and avoiding 0.3% or higher; qualifying bulk marketing requires one-click unsubscribe. Treat these as provider-specific controls, not a promise of inbox placement. [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)

An internal hard-bounce pause threshold of 2% is a suggested operational guardrail, not an official universal standard. Investigate complaints and repeated deferrals even at lower volumes. Maintain a central suppression list and stop immediately on objections.

For UK corporate outreach, the ICO distinguishes corporate subscribers from sole traders and certain partnerships; personal-data processing still requires an appropriate UK GDPR basis, transparency, and respect for objections. For US commercial email, apply CAN-SPAM requirements including truthful headers/subjects, clear commercial identification, a valid postal address, and a functioning opt-out. Do not assume UK rules transfer to continental Europe. [ICO B2B guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/), [FTC business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

**12. Proof, trust, and risks to resolve**

| Risk | Severity / confidence | Suggested owner | Mitigation and timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product performance does not improve the buyer’s workflow | High; actual performance unverified | Infratex technical lead | Representative benchmark and holdout; before production proposal |
| Security or private deployment cannot meet requirements | High; requirements and evidence unknown | Infratex founder/security owner | Document hosting, retention, deletion, training use, subprocessors, access controls, and actual deployment boundaries; before private data |
| Outbound generates low-value free users | High; supported by low entry pricing, actual cohort economics unknown | Growency + Infratex founder | Qualify recurring routed volume and budget; first call |
| Free evaluations consume engineering capacity | Medium/high; planning risk | Infratex technical lead | Fixed scope, weekly cap, effort logging, paid expansion; before launch |
| Unsupported benchmark or customer claims reduce trust | High; public evidence discrepancy identified | Infratex founder | Reproduce results and secure customer permission; before claims enter copy |
| Partner conversations never become projects | Medium; untested hypothesis | Growency partner lead | Qualify live project, owner, budget, and timing; initial conversation |

Prepare a small proof pack: one representative sample per initial vertical, a transparent scorecard, a cost example, integration instructions, and a verified security/data-flow summary. If claiming private deployment, specify which components and models run privately; a self-deployable parser alone does not prove an entirely private retrieval/answering stack.

The campaign landing page should match the document workflow, show a real input/output example, describe the benchmark, and make the next step explicit. Keep technical depth available for evaluation. Publish named customer results only with permission; report the sample size, document class, metric, and limitations.

**13. Immediate founder briefing and next actions**

1. **Confirm the real commercial baseline.** Suggested owner: Infratex founder; due: before launch. Establish paying customers, strongest document class, revenue per account, margin by method, and delivery capacity. Outcome: a viable acquisition target. Linked risks: economics and capacity.
2. **Reproduce the strongest product result.** Suggested owner: technical lead; due: week 1. Resolve benchmark provenance and build a representative evaluation. Outcome: supportable sales evidence. Linked risks: performance and trust.
3. **Approve the bounded offer and data-handling process.** Suggested owner: founder + technical/security owner; due: week 1. Outcome: an evaluation that can be delivered within known cost and data constraints. Linked risks: security and capacity.
4. **Build the first 150-account cohort.** Suggested owner: Growency researcher; due: week 2. Attach a source and relevant owner to each account. Outcome: three clean segment tests. Linked risks: fit and low-value demand.
5. **Run one reply-to-pilot process.** Suggested owner: Growency campaign owner + founder; due: before first send. Outcome: prompt follow-up, defined qualification, clear ownership, and measurable progression. Linked risks: conversion and staff effort.
6. **Make the segment decision from completed cohorts.** Suggested owner: Growency strategy lead + founder; due: weeks 5–6. Use qualified opportunities, pilot conversion, projected contribution, and effort—not raw reply count. Outcome: a defensible choice about where to invest next.

The smallest useful next internal session is a founder review of the strongest existing customer/workload, actual unit costs, and the proposed benchmark offer. That information could change the leading segment and commercial target without requiring a complete strategy restart.
