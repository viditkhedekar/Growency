1. What Posting Machine does
Posting Machine helps B2B founders turn LinkedIn into a revenue-generating channel. We are not selling "more posts" as the end product; the outcome we care about is relevant buyer attention, qualified conversations, and pipeline.
Our workflow: turn founder and company context into content, learn what resonates with the ICP, increase relevant engagement, and use that attention to create qualified sales opportunities.
Core thesis: the ideal customer already has product-market fit, but has not yet found content-market fit on LinkedIn.
What we are not: a solution for companies with no customers, no clear ICP, or no functioning GTM motion.

3. Pricing & commercial structure
Current offer: $499/month. This is discounted early-stage pricing and should be positioned as the current entry offer, not as our long-term price ceiling.
Standard structure: $499/month with a three-month contract.
The first month is base-fee only. Do not introduce or promise performance-based fees for month one.
From month two onward, we may add a performance-based component on top of the monthly base fee when there is a clear, mutually agreed definition of performance.
Potential performance metrics may include qualified ICP engagement, qualified conversations, meetings, or customers generated, depending on the account and what can be measured reliably.
The agency should not position performance pricing as guaranteed or standardized. Treat it as a potential expansion of the commercial model after the first month.
For outbound, lead with the business outcome and customer proof. Pricing can be introduced as: $499/month on a three-month engagement, with the possibility of aligning part of future pricing to performance once we have baseline data.

6. Founder LinkedIn profile
Follower range: 500-10,000. The strongest sweet spot is 1,000-5,000.
500-1,000: include when company fit is excellent and the founder shows some activity.
1,000-5,000: highest priority.
5,000-10,000: include if content is inconsistent, generic, weakly engaged, or not generating pipeline.
10,000+: generally exclude unless there is a clear content-to-revenue gap.
Ideal current LinkedIn behavior
Posts roughly 1-4 times per month or irregularly.
Has expertise and credibility but weak consistency.
Some posts perform well, showing latent channel potential.
Content is dominated by company announcements rather than buyer-relevant insight.
Reasonable follower base but limited ICP engagement.
No obvious mature content team, creator operation, or repeatable LinkedIn inbound engine.
7. Buyer / LinkedIn fit
This is a hard qualification dimension. The company should sell to professional buyers who actively use LinkedIn and can be identified at scale.
Strong buyer examples: CEOs, CFOs, CTOs, CIOs, CISOs, CROs, CMOs, CHROs, VPs, engineering leaders, finance leaders, HR leaders, recruiters, healthcare executives, operations leaders, logistics leaders, security professionals, legal professionals, and SaaS operators.
Strong category examples: enterprise AI, developer tools, cybersecurity, data/cloud infrastructure, HR/recruiting tech, sales/marketing tech, fintech infrastructure, B2B healthtech, legaltech, logistics/supply-chain software, vertical SaaS, compliance, risk, procurement, and operations software.
8. Economic fit
The current $499/month offer should be easy to justify economically, and the account should have enough upside to support $1,000-$3,000/month over time if LinkedIn creates qualified opportunities or pipeline.
Ideal: $10K+ ACV, mid-market or enterprise sales, founder-led or sales-assisted motion.
Acceptable: $5K-$10K ACV with strong LTV or fast deal velocity.
Lower priority: below $5K ACV.
Avoid businesses where many incremental customers would be required to justify even the current $499/month fee or where a future $1K-$3K relationship would never make economic sense.
9. Exclusions
Pre-product, idea-stage, or obviously pre-PMF companies.
No paying customers or unclear ICP.
Consumer apps, gaming, or primarily local SMB products.
Products whose buyers are rarely active on LinkedIn.
Very low-ticket products.
Professional creators / LinkedIn influencers.
Founders posting daily with consistently strong engagement and obvious mature content systems.
Founders whose personal brand is already a major acquisition engine.

11. Qualification tiers
Tier A - Contact first: strong fit across PMF, economics, LinkedIn-active ICP, founder follower range, content gap, and founder-led GTM relevance.
Tier B - Good target: strong overall fit with one or two uncertain dimensions, such as ACV or PMF evidence.
Tier C - Borderline: only use if needed after higher-quality pools are exhausted. Do not pad the list with weak accounts.
12. Outbound execution
Use a dual-channel motion wherever possible: cold email + LinkedIn. The agency should optimize for qualified conversations, not send volume.
Prioritize founders where both a work email and LinkedIn profile are available.
Verify emails before sending and track deliverability.
Use account-specific personalization rather than generic compliments.
Use the same core hypothesis across channels, but do not duplicate the exact message verbatim.
Prioritize Tier A accounts first and feed campaign learnings back into list-building.
Good personalization signals
Recent Seed or Series A raise.
Recent product launch.
Hiring sales/GTM roles.
Major customer announcement.
Founder recently posted about a buyer problem.
Founder has strong expertise but posts inconsistently.
One recent post performed unusually well.
Content is mostly company announcements.
Company is expanding GTM or entering a new vertical.
Avoid: generic personalization such as "Love what you are building." The opening should contain a concrete reason this founder was selected.
