# Writing research

Reviewed 24 October 2026. These sources inform editing choices; none establishes that an email will be perceived as human or guarantees responses.

- [Nielsen Norman Group, ChatGPT and Tone: Avoid Sounding Like a Robot](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chatgpt-and-tone/), 8 March 2024. Its qualitative examples favor providing existing copy over relying on a single tone adjective, which can produce exaggerated language. Apply this by keeping a few short voice examples in the skill. Do not generate multiple alternatives during bulk drafting, despite that being another suggestion in the article.
- [Paul Graham, Write Simply](https://paulgraham.com/simply.html), March 2021. Editorial advice to use ordinary words and simple sentences. Apply this as a writing preference, not experimental evidence about email conversion or AI authorship.
- [Gong Labs, The best sales email tips of 2021](https://www.gong.io/blog/these-are-the-best-sales-email-tips-youll-read-in-2021), published 7 April 2021, page updated 6 March 2026. Reports observational analysis of over 300,000 North American B2B technology sales emails; success meant a meeting booked within ten days. Supports keeping messages concise and focused on the recipient. It does not test perceived AI authorship or prove causation. Preserve the user's approved proof points and 12-minute CTA rather than changing them to match this article.

Implementation judgment: one concrete detail, a restrained reaction, and removal of stock praise should fit the user's desired voice better. Avoid invented experiences and fake mistakes. Test any future conversion claims separately.
